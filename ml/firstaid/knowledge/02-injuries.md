# Injuries

## fall
critical: no
title: A fall | Kuanguka
keywords: fell, fall, fallen, found on the floor, slipped, tripped, fell down, fell out of bed, can't get up, fell in the bathroom, fell on the stairs, ameanguka, ameteleza, ameanguka bafuni, ameanguka kitandani, hawezi kuinuka, nimemkuta sakafuni, ameanguka chooni, ameanguka ngazini

### en
intro: After a fall, an older person can be badly hurt without it being obvious. Do not rush to lift them.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they hit their head, were knocked out even briefly, take blood thinners, cannot move a leg, have severe pain in the hip, back or neck, are confused, or cannot get up.
steps:
- Stay calm, check the area is safe, and talk to them. Ask what happened and where it hurts.
- Check they are awake and breathing normally. If not, follow the unresponsive or CPR steps.
- Look for injuries: bleeding, a head bump, pain in the hip or groin, or a leg that looks shorter or turned outwards, which can mean a broken hip.
- If they are hurt or cannot get up, keep them still, put a pillow under the head only if there is no neck pain, cover them with a blanket and wait for help.
- If they are not hurt and want to get up: help them roll onto their side, then onto hands and knees, crawl to a sturdy chair, put their hands on the seat, bring one foot forward and push up to turn and sit.
- Let them rest, then check for dizziness, new pain or confusion over the next hours.
avoid:
- Do not lift them by yourself, you can injure both of you.
- Do not give food or drink if they might have a broken bone, in case they need surgery.
after: Tell the family and their doctor about every fall, even if they seem fine. Falls can be a sign of illness, low blood pressure or medicine side effects.

## fracture
critical: no
title: Broken bone | Kuvunjika mfupa
keywords: broken bone, fracture, broken arm, broken leg, broken wrist, broken hip, hip fracture, bone sticking out, leg is bent wrong, swollen and deformed, cracked bone, kuvunjika mfupa, amevunjika mguu, amevunjika mkono, nyonga imevunjika, mfupa umetoka nje, mguu umepinda, kifundo kimevunjika

### en
intro: Signs of a broken bone are pain, swelling, bruising, a limb at a strange angle, or not being able to move or put weight on it.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 for a suspected broken hip, thigh, pelvis, neck or back, a bone poking through the skin, or a limb that is cold, pale or numb below the injury. For other breaks, take them to hospital.
steps:
- Keep them still and support the injured part in the position you found it, using pillows, rolled blankets or clothing.
- For an arm, if they can bend it, support it in a sling made from a scarf or cloth.
- If the bone has broken the skin, cover the wound with a clean dressing and press around it, not on the bone.
- Put an ice pack wrapped in a cloth on the area for up to 20 minutes to reduce swelling.
- Watch for signs of shock: pale, cold, sweaty skin or confusion.
avoid:
- Do not try to straighten the limb or push a bone back in.
- Do not give food or drink, they may need surgery.
- Do not move them if you suspect a hip, back or neck injury, unless they are in danger.
after: Older people often break a hip or wrist in a fall. Even if they can still walk, persistent pain needs an X-ray.

## sprain
critical: no
title: Sprain, strain or bruise | Kuteguka, mshtuko wa msuli au mchubuko
keywords: sprain, sprained ankle, twisted ankle, muscle strain, strained a muscle, strained his, strained her, pulled muscle, swollen ankle, bruise, bruised, twisted knee, kuteguka, ameteguka, kifundo kimeteguka, msuli umevutika, uvimbe wa kifundo, mchubuko, amejigonga

### en
intro: Twisting a joint or pulling a muscle causes pain, swelling and bruising. Most improve with simple care.
steps:
- Rest: stop using the injured part and help them sit or lie down.
- Ice: put an ice pack or cold wet cloth wrapped in a towel on it for 15 to 20 minutes, every 2 to 3 hours for the first 2 days.
- Compression: wrap it with an elastic bandage, firm but not tight. Loosen it if toes or fingers go cold, blue or numb.
- Elevation: raise the injured part on pillows, above the level of the heart if possible.
- Paracetamol can help with pain if it is safe with their other medicines.
avoid:
- Do not put ice directly on the skin.
- Do not use heat, massage or rub the area for the first 2 days.
after: See a doctor if they cannot put weight on it, the pain is severe, it looks deformed, there is numbness, or it is not better in a few days. In older people after a fall, it may be a fracture.

## head_injury

### en
intro: Most bumps to the head are minor, but older people and anyone on blood thinners can bleed inside the skull, sometimes hours later.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they were knocked out even briefly, are confused or very drowsy, vomit more than once, have a severe or worsening headache, have a seizure, have blood or clear fluid from the ears or nose, have weakness, slurred speech or vision problems. Anyone on blood thinners who hits their head must go to hospital today.

- Help them sit down and rest.
- Hold a cold pack wrapped in a cloth on the bump for 10 to 20 minutes.
- If there is a cut, press on it with a clean cloth to stop the bleeding.

- Watch for any of the danger signs above and call for help straight away if they appear.
avoid:
- Do not leave them alone for the first 24 hours.
- Do not give alcohol or sleeping tablets.
- If you suspect a neck injury, do not move them.
after: Tell the family and the doctor about the head injury, even if it seemed small.

## spinal_injury

### en
intro: Suspect a neck or back injury after a fall from a height, a hard blow to the head, a road accident, or if they have neck or back pain, tingling, numbness or weakness in the arms or legs.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 now.
steps:
- Tell them not to move.

- Keep checking their breathing. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.
- If you must turn them, for example because they are vomiting, roll them with helpers, keeping the head, neck and body in line.
avoid:
- Do not move them unless they are in danger.
- Do not let them sit up or turn their head.
after: Tell the paramedics how the injury happened and whether they could move their arms and legs.

intro: Small cuts and grazes usually heal well if they are cleaned and covered.
steps:

avoid:
- Do not put cotton wool directly on the wound, fibres stick.

after: Ask a nurse to check it, and pad furniture edges and keep the skin moisturised to prevent more tears.

### en
intro: Most nosebleeds can be stopped at home.
steps:
- Sit them down and lean them forward, not back.
- Pinch the soft part of the nose, just below the bony bridge, firmly for 10 to 15 minutes without letting go.
- Ask them to breathe through the mouth and spit out any blood.
- A cold cloth on the bridge of the nose can help.
- When it stops, they should rest and avoid blowing the nose, bending down or lifting heavy things for 24 hours.
avoid:
- Do not tilt the head back, blood runs down the throat and can cause vomiting or choking.
- Do not let them lie down.
after: Get medical help if it lasts more than 20 minutes, is very heavy, followed a head injury, or they take blood thinners and it will not stop.

## burns
critical: no
title: Burns and scalds | Kuungua na moto au maji ya moto
keywords: burn, burnt, burned, scald, scalded, hot water burn, boiling water, fire burn, steam burn, chemical burn, blister from burn, burnt by stove, burnt by jiko, ameungua, ameungua na moto, ameungua na maji ya moto, ameungua na chai, jiko limemchoma, malengelenge ya moto, ameungua na mafuta

### en
intro: Cooling a burn quickly with water reduces the damage. Older people's skin burns more deeply.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 or go to hospital if the burn is bigger than the person's hand, is on the face, hands, feet, genitals or a joint, looks white, leathery or charred, was caused by electricity or chemicals, or they breathed in smoke.
steps:
- Move them away from the heat source.
- Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. This still helps up to 3 hours after the burn.
- Take off rings, watches and clothing near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin.
- After cooling, cover it loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy cloth.
