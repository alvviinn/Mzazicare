## low_blood_sugar
critical: no
title: Low blood sugar (diabetes) | Sukari kushuka (kisukari)
keywords: low blood sugar, low sugar, hypo, hypoglycaemia, hypoglycemia, sugar dropped, diabetic shaking sweating, diabetic confused, sugar is low, glucose low, sukari imeshuka, sukari iko chini, sukari imepungua, mgonjwa wa kisukari anatetemeka, kisukari anatokwa jasho

### en
intro: People with diabetes who take insulin or diabetes tablets can have low blood sugar. Signs are shaking, sweating, paleness, hunger, a fast heartbeat, confusion, irritability, unusual behaviour or drowsiness.
urgent: If they are very drowsy, unconscious or cannot swallow safely, call 999 or 112, put them in the recovery position and give nothing by mouth.
steps:
- If they are awake and can swallow, sit them down.
- Give fast sugar straight away: half a glass (150 ml) of fruit juice or a non-diet soda, or 3 to 4 teaspoons of sugar stirred into water, or glucose tablets.
- Wait 10 to 15 minutes and check their sugar with their meter if they have one.
- If they are not better, give fast sugar again. You can repeat up to 3 times.
- Once they feel better, give a starchy snack or their next meal, such as bread, biscuits or ugali.
- If they do not improve after 3 rounds, or get worse, call 999 or 112.
avoid:
- Do not give anything by mouth if they are drowsy or cannot swallow safely.
- Do not give chocolate or fatty food as the first treatment, it works too slowly.
- Do not leave them alone until they have recovered.
after: Tell the family and their doctor, as their diabetes medicine or meals may need changing.

## high_blood_sugar
critical: no
title: High blood sugar (diabetes) | Sukari kupanda sana (kisukari)
keywords: high blood sugar, high sugar, hyperglycaemia, hyperglycemia, sugar is high, glucose high, meter says hi, very thirsty diabetic, sugar reading 20, ketones, sukari iko juu, sukari imepanda, sukari nyingi mwilini, kiu sana na kukojoa sana

### en
intro: High blood sugar causes thirst, passing a lot of urine, tiredness, blurred vision and headaches. Very high sugar can become dangerous.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 or go to hospital if the meter reads "HI" or above about 16.7 mmol/L (300 mg/dL) and they are vomiting, very drowsy, confused, breathing fast and deep, or their breath smells fruity.
steps:
- Check their sugar with their meter if they have one, and write down the reading and time.
- Give plenty of water or other sugar-free drinks.
- Help them follow their diabetes plan and take their usual medicine as prescribed.
- Contact their doctor or clinic today for advice if the sugar stays high.
- Recheck the sugar in 2 hours and watch for the danger signs above.
avoid:
- Do not give sugary drinks or sweets.
- Do not skip or change their insulin or tablets unless the doctor says so.
after: Share the readings with the family and the doctor so treatment can be adjusted.

## asthma
critical: no
title: Asthma attack | Shambulio la pumu
keywords: asthma, asthma attack, wheezing, inhaler, blue inhaler, salbutamol, tight chest wheeze, pumu, shambulio la pumu, anapumua kwa mluzi, kifaa cha kupuliza, inhaler yake

### en
intro: In an asthma attack the airways tighten, causing wheezing, coughing, a tight chest and breathlessness.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they are too breathless to speak, their lips are turning blue, they are exhausted or drowsy, or the inhaler is not helping.
steps:
- Help them sit upright, leaning slightly forward. Keep calm and reassure them.
- Help them use their reliever inhaler, usually blue (salbutamol): 1 puff every 30 to 60 seconds, up to 10 puffs. Use a spacer if they have one.
- If they are not better after 10 puffs, or are getting worse, call 999 or 112.
- If help has not arrived after 10 minutes, repeat up to 10 puffs.
avoid:
- Do not lie them down.
- Do not leave them alone.
after: Even if the attack settles, they should see their doctor within 2 days to review their asthma treatment.

- Msaidie atumie inhaler yake ya kupunguza pumu, kwa kawaida ya bluu (salbutamol): pafu 1 kila sekunde 30 hadi 60, hadi pafu 10. Tumia spacer kama anayo.

## breathing_difficulty
critical: no
title: Difficulty breathing | Kupumua kwa shida
keywords: shortness of breath, short of breath, breathless, difficulty breathing, can't catch breath, struggling to breathe, copd, breathing fast, oxygen, anapumua kwa shida, anakosa pumzi, hapati hewa, pumzi inakata, anahema sana, kupumua kwa shida

### en
intro: Breathlessness in an older person can come from the lungs, the heart, an infection or anxiety. Sudden or severe breathlessness is an emergency.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if it came on suddenly, is severe, they have chest pain, blue or grey lips, cannot finish a sentence, are confused or drowsy, or cannot lie flat when they normally can.
steps:
- Help them sit upright, leaning forward with arms resting on a table or their knees.
- Loosen tight clothing and open a window for fresh air.
- If they have a prescribed inhaler or home oxygen, help them use it as directed.
- Encourage slow breathing: in through the nose, out slowly through pursed lips.
- Stay with them and keep checking how they are breathing and how alert they are.
avoid:
- Do not lie them flat.
- Do not allow smoking or open flames near home oxygen.
after: Tell their doctor today about any new or worsening breathlessness, even if it settles.

## fainting
critical: no
title: Fainting | Kuzirai
keywords: fainted, faint, fainting, feeling faint, dizzy and passed out, blacked out, collapsed briefly, light headed, amezirai, anazirai, kuzirai, anahisi kuzirai, kizunguzungu na kuanguka, giza machoni

### en
intro: Fainting is a short loss of consciousness when blood flow to the brain drops. People usually wake within a minute.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they do not wake within a minute, are injured, have chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat or breathlessness, fainted during activity, or it is the first time in an older person.
steps:
- If they feel faint, help them lie down, or sit with the head between the knees.
- If they have fainted, lay them on their back and raise their legs about 30 cm on a chair or pillows.
- Loosen tight clothing and let fresh air in.
- When they wake, keep them lying down for a few minutes, then help them sit up slowly.
- Give sips of water once they are fully awake.
- If they stay unresponsive but are breathing, put them in the recovery position.
avoid:
- Do not sit them up or stand them up quickly.
- Do not splash water on their face or give drinks while drowsy.
after: In older people fainting can be a sign of a heart problem, low blood pressure or a medicine side effect, so tell their doctor.

## poisoning
critical: yes
title: Poisoning | Sumu
keywords: poisoning, poisoned, swallowed poison, drank paraffin, drank bleach, swallowed chemicals, pesticide, rat poison, ate something poisonous, mushroom poisoning, charcoal fumes, carbon monoxide, jiko fumes, smoke inhalation, sumu, amekunywa sumu, amekunywa mafuta ya taa, amekunywa jiki, dawa ya panya, dawa ya wadudu, moshi wa jiko, amevuta moshi wa makaa

### en
intro: Poisoning can come from swallowing chemicals, pesticides, too much medicine, poisonous plants, or breathing in fumes, such as from a charcoal jiko in a closed room.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 now.
steps:
- For fumes or smoke: get them into fresh air straight away, open doors and windows, and turn off or remove the jiko or heater, without putting yourself at risk.
- Find out what they took or touched, how much and when. Keep the container, packet or plant to show the medics.
- If they are unresponsive but breathing, put them in the recovery position. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR, using a face shield if the poison is on their mouth.
- If a chemical is on the skin or in the eyes, rinse with lots of running water for 20 minutes and remove contaminated clothing.
- If chemicals burned the lips, they can rinse their mouth and spit, without swallowing.
avoid:
- Do not make them vomit.
- Do not give salt water, milk or oil. Only give drinks if a doctor or poison expert tells you to.
- Never use a charcoal jiko indoors with doors and windows closed.
after: Take the container or a photo of the label to hospital.

## medication_mistake
critical: no
title: Too much or wrong medicine | Dawa nyingi au dawa isiyo sahihi

### en
intro: Taking a double dose, the wrong medicine or someone else's medicine is common with older people. Many mistakes are harmless, but some medicines are dangerous in extra doses.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 straight away if they are drowsy, confused, breathing slowly, have chest pain, a very slow or fast heartbeat, a seizure, or are unresponsive, or if they took a large amount on purpose.
steps:
- Stay calm and find out exactly which medicine, how much and when. Keep the packets and the medicine list.
- Call their doctor, the clinic or a pharmacist now for advice, or 999 or 112 if you cannot reach them.
- Watch them closely for the next hours for drowsiness, confusion, vomiting or changes in breathing.
- If they become unresponsive but are breathing, put them in the recovery position.
- For a missed dose: check the leaflet or ask the pharmacist. Usually you take it when remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.
avoid:
- Do not make them vomit.
- Never give a double dose to make up for a missed one, unless a doctor tells you to.
after: A weekly pill organiser and a written medicine chart help prevent repeat mistakes. Tell the family about the mistake.

## heat_illness
critical: no
title: Heat exhaustion and heatstroke | Uchovu wa joto na kiharusi cha joto

### en
intro: Older people overheat easily. Heat exhaustion causes headache, dizziness, heavy sweating, cramps, nausea and a fast pulse. Heatstroke is an emergency: hot skin, confusion, fits or unconsciousness.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they are confused, have a seizure, are unresponsive, have a very high temperature (40 °C or more), or do not improve within 30 minutes.
steps:
- Move them to a cool, shady place and help them lie down with legs slightly raised.
- Remove extra clothing.
- Cool the skin: spray or sponge with cool water and fan them. Cold packs wrapped in cloth in the armpits and neck help.
- If they are alert, give sips of cool water or oral rehydration solution (ORS).
- Keep cooling and watch them closely.
avoid:
- Do not give alcohol, coffee or very sugary drinks.
- Do not give anything to drink if they are confused or drowsy.
after: Keep them cool and drinking regularly on hot days, and check on them often.

## hypothermia
critical: no
title: Too cold (hypothermia) | Baridi kali mwilini (hypothermia)
keywords: hypothermia, very cold, freezing, shivering a lot, cold and confused, body is cold, cold night, baridi kali, anatetemeka kwa baridi, mwili ni baridi sana, amepigwa na baridi, baridi usiku

### en
intro: Older people can get dangerously cold, even indoors on cold nights in the highlands. Signs are shivering, cold pale skin, slurred speech, confusion, clumsiness and drowsiness.
urgent: Call 999 or 112 if they are drowsy, confused, have stopped shivering while still cold, or are breathing slowly.
steps:
- Move them to a warm room, out of the wind.
- Replace wet clothing with dry clothing.
- Wrap them in blankets, including the head, and put a blanket underneath if they are on the floor.
- If they are alert and can swallow, give warm sweet drinks and some food.
- Warm the room, and stay with them until their temperature and alertness are back to normal.
avoid:
- Do not put them in a hot bath, or put hot water bottles or heaters directly against the skin.
- Do not rub their arms and legs.
- Do not give alcohol.

### sw
intro: Sanduku lenye vifaa vya kutosha, lililowekwa mahali kila mtu anapoweza kulipata, huokoa muda wakati wa dharura.
steps:
- Glavu za kutupa, pedi za gozi safi na bendeji za saizi tofauti, na plasta.
- Bendeji za kunyooka, bendeji ya pembetatu kwa kombeo, na tepu ya kunata.
- Mkasi safi, koleo dogo na pini za usalama.
- Maji ya chumvi (saline) au maji safi ya kusuuza, na dawa ya kuua vijidudu kama povidone-iodine.
- Kipimajoto, na mashine ya sukari na ya shinikizo la damu kama mgonjwa anazitumia.
- Pakiti za ORS, paracetamol, na dawa zozote za dharura alizoandikiwa, kama inhaler au sindano ya adrenaline.
- Tochi, blanketi ya foil, na kadi yenye nambari za dharura (999, 112, Red Cross 1199), nambari za familia, dawa za mgonjwa, mizio na magonjwa yake.
avoid:
- Usiweke dawa zilizopitwa na muda. Kagua sanduku kila miezi 3.
after: Waonyeshe wote nyumbani mahali sanduku lilipo.
